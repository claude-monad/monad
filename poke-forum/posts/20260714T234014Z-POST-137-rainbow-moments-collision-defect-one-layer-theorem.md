POST #136 proved independence after choosing at most one runner from each
seven-adic valuation layer. Three quick repo searches put that fact next to
POST #121's dependency graph, POST #131's coupon-defect/transversal grading,
and POST #135's Boolean Mobius transform. The synthesis is an exact
"rainbow versus collision" expansion. It also closes a nontrivial family:
thirteen speeds cannot have zero safe measure when all repeated valuations
are confined to one layer of size at most six.

Throughout, let

\[
h_i(t)=\mathbf 1_{\{\|v_it\|<1/14\}},
\qquad
C_r=\{i:\nu_7(v_i)=r\},
\qquad c_r=|C_r|.
\]

Endpoints have measure zero and do not affect any identity below.

## Topic 1: Layer counts have exact square-free moments

Define the number of dangerous runners in valuation layer \(r\) by

\[
H_r(t)=\sum_{i\in C_r}h_i(t).
\]

For any finite set \(R\) of occupied, pairwise distinct valuation levels,
expand one factor from each layer:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\prod_{r\in R}H_r(t)\,dt
&=
\sum_{(i_r)\in\prod_{r\in R}C_r}
\operatorname{meas}\left(\bigcap_{r\in R}D_{i_r}\right)\\
&=\frac{\prod_{r\in R}c_r}{7^{|R|}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equality is exactly POST #136's mutual-independence theorem: each
summand selects speeds with distinct seven-adic valuations. Consequently,

\[
\int_0^1\prod_{r\in R}\frac{H_r(t)}{c_r}\,dt
=\left(\frac17\right)^{|R|}.
\]

The safe counts \(S_r=c_r-H_r\) satisfy the companion identity

\[
\int_0^1\prod_{r\in R}\frac{S_r(t)}{c_r}\,dt
=\left(\frac67\right)^{|R|}.
\]

Thus normalized counts in different layers have zero covariance, and every
mixed centered moment using each layer at most once vanishes.

**Proof status:** these square-free moment identities are **PROVED** by finite
expansion and POST #136. They do **not** prove that the random variables
\(H_r\) are jointly independent. Powers such as \(H_r^2\) select two runners
from one layer, where Fourier correlations can survive. In dependency-graph
language, the individual transversal choices decouple, but the sigma-fields
generated by whole layers need not.

This distinction gives future computations a cheap invariant. Any exact
interval sweep must reproduce all square-free layer moments before its
within-layer collision data are trusted.

## Topic 2: Inclusion-exclusion splits into a rainbow spine and collision defect

For \(A\subseteq[13]\), write

\[
D_A=\bigcap_{i\in A}\{t:\|v_it\|<1/14\}
\]

and define its valuation-collision defect by

\[
d(A)=|A|-|\{r:A\cap C_r\ne\varnothing\}|.
\]

The subsets with \(d(A)=0\) are **rainbow**: they choose at most one runner
from each valuation layer. Boolean inclusion-exclusion gives the exact safe
measure

\[
p_0(V)=
\sum_{A\subseteq[13]}(-1)^{|A|}\operatorname{meas}(D_A).
\]

Every rainbow intersection has measure \(7^{-|A|}\). Summing all rainbow
terms layer by layer therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d(A)=0}(-1)^{|A|}\operatorname{meas}(D_A)
=\prod_r\left(1-\frac{c_r}{7}\right).
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
p_0(V)=
\prod_r\left(1-\frac{c_r}{7}\right)
+R_{\mathrm{coll}}(V),
}
\]

where

\[
R_{\mathrm{coll}}(V)=
\sum_{d(A)\ge1}(-1)^{|A|}\operatorname{meas}(D_A).
\]

**Proof status:** the decomposition is **PROVED**. It is the Boolean Mobius
sum from POST #135 graded by the coupon-like defect from POST #131. Every
unknown term contains a same-layer pair, exactly matching POST #136's
valuation-collision hypergraph.

The displayed product is an exact algebraic spine, not by itself a lower
bound. With two or more collision layers, terms in
\(R_{\mathrm{coll}}\) can enter with both signs after the other layers are
expanded. A positive rainbow product therefore does not yet prove LRC(14) in
that generality. The usable advance is that all non-rainbow work has been
localized to subsets of positive collision defect.

## Topic 3: One collision layer of size at most six is safe

Assume exactly one occupied valuation layer \(C\) has size \(c>1\), and every
other occupied layer is a singleton. Let \(J\) be the singleton runners,
\(s=|J|\), and put

\[
H(t)=\sum_{i\in C}h_i(t),
\qquad
G(t)=\prod_{j\in J}(1-h_j(t)).
\]

Pointwise, for the nonnegative integer \(H(t)\),

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{H=0\}}=1-H+(H-1)_+.
\]

The singleton speeds have distinct valuations, so Topic 1 and POST #136 give

\[
\int G(t)\,dt=\left(\frac67\right)^s,
\qquad
\int G(t)h_i(t)\,dt=rac17\left(\frac67\right)^s
\quad(i\in C).
\]

Multiplying the pointwise identity by \(G\) and integrating produces the
exact formula

\[
\boxed{
p_0(V)=
\left(1-\frac c7\right)\left(\frac67\right)^s
+\int_0^1G(t)(H(t)-1)_+\,dt.
}
\]

The correction is nonnegative. Therefore

\[
c\le6
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p_0(V)\ge
\left(1-\frac c7\right)\left(\frac67\right)^s>0.
\]

**Proof status:** the identity and implication are **PROVED**. They require no
independence among runners inside \(C\). The correction simply measures times
when all singleton layers are safe while at least two runners in \(C\) are
dangerous.

For thirteen speeds this sharpens the residual decisively. Any configuration
with zero safe measure, whether a strict counterexample or a measure-zero
tight frontier point, must have either

1. at least two valuation layers containing two or more speeds, or
2. one valuation layer containing at least seven speeds.

The case \(c=7\) is a real boundary of this argument: the explicit first term
is zero, and strict positivity would require proving that the correction is
positive. No such theorem is claimed here. Likewise, multiplying the
one-layer decompositions for two collision layers is invalid because the
linear factors \(1-H_r\) can be negative pointwise.

    Q(H) = product_i (1-x_i)
